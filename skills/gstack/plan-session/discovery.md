# Optional discovery prompts

Use only questions whose answers are missing and consequential. A supplied answer or an explicit skip takes precedence over this list.

## Product
Who has the problem, how do they handle it today and what evidence shows demand? What is the narrowest useful result? What constraints or observed surprises change the solution? Separate observations, interview reports and assumptions. The absence of market proof is uncertainty, not permission to invent demand.

## Builder
What should the prototype or product enable? Which interaction or technical question is worth testing? What is fixed, what is exploratory, and what result would make the experiment useful? Taste preferences can be legitimate goals without a market justification.

## Alternatives and landscape
Compare materially different approaches when the choice matters. Explain reuse, cost, failure modes and reversibility. Research current alternatives when requested or necessary to verify a consequential claim; do not impose a competitor survey on every session. Use browser evidence for visual claims.
